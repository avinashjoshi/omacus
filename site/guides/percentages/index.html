Percentages, the way you'd say them

Discounts, tax, tips, raises and "what percent is that?" all have a natural phrasing. Omacus understands them, so 15% off $1,299 gives $1,104.15 without reaching for a formula.

Omacus is a free notepad calculator for Linux, macOS and Windows. Each line is calculated as you type, so you can try a discount, add tax on the next line and see both answers at once.

Of, on and off

of takes a share: 20% of 340 is 68. on adds it, for tax or a markup: 5% on $200 is $210. off takes it away, for a discount: 15% off $1,299 is $1,104.15.

Adding or taking off a percentage

Writing + or - a percentage means a percentage of that amount, the way a shop or a receipt uses it. You can name what it's for after it:

Questions

What does $100 - 20% give?

$80. A percentage added to or taken from an amount is a percentage of that amount, like on a receipt.

How do I calculate what percent one number is of another?

Write 6 is what % of 120 or 6 as % of 120. Omacus answers 5%.

How do I add sales tax or VAT?

Use on or +: 5% on $200 or $200 + 5% tax. Both give $210.

How do I find the original price before a percentage?

Write 40 is 20% of what (200), or 5% of what is 6 (120).
