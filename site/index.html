    // private by design

    Your pages stay yours.

    No account, no cloud, no tracking in the app. What you write never leaves your device unless you send it.

    On your computer

    Pages are plain text files in a folder on your disk. Search them, sync them, back them up; they're yours.

    In your browser

    The web app saves pages in that browser, on that device. Nothing is uploaded, and there's no server that could see them.

    In a share link

    The page rides after the #, the part of a link browsers never send to a server. Only people you give it to can read it.

    The only things Omacus fetches are exchange rates, and on the desktop, a check for new versions. Neither carries anything you've written. All of it, in plain words

    // questions

    Good questions.

    What is Omacus?

    Omacus is a free, open-source notepad calculator for Linux, macOS and Windows. You write calculations in plain words, one per line, and each answer appears beside its line as you type. It understands units, currencies, time zones, percentages, variables and running totals, and it was made first for Omarchy.

    Is Omacus free?

    Yes. Omacus is free and open source under the MIT license. The app has no account, no ads and no tracking. This website only counts visits, with Cloudflare's cookieless analytics.

    Which systems does it run on?

    Omarchy and any 64-bit x86 Linux (as a .deb, .rpm, tarball or one-line installer), macOS on Apple Silicon and Intel, and 64-bit Windows.

    How do I convert time zones?

    Type the time and both zones, like 6pm PST in Tokyo. Omacus answers 10:00 AM JST (next day). It knows abbreviations, cities and countries, and adjusts for daylight saving.

    Does it work offline?

    Yes. Everything is calculated on your computer. The internet is only used to refresh exchange rates and to check for a new version (on launch and every few hours), and the last rates are cached so currency math keeps working offline.

    How is it different from a regular calculator?

    A regular calculator shows one number at a time. Omacus keeps a whole page: every line keeps its answer, you can add notes beside the math, name values and reuse them, and changing a line updates everything below it. Pages are saved as you type.

    Can I use it from the terminal?

    Yes. omacus '$100 in EUR' prints the answer, and omacus - evaluates a whole page from standard input, one result per line.

    Where are my pages stored?

    As plain text files on your own disk, so you can search, sync or back them up like any other notes. Nothing is uploaded.
